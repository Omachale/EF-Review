so_auxTo say you are the same: So + auxiliary + I after a positive sentence (A: I love cats. B: So do I.), Neither + auxiliary + I after a negative sentence (A: I can’t swim. B: Neither can I.).
so_tenseThe auxiliary depends on the verb in the first sentence: do / does (present simple), did (past simple), am / is / are, was / were, can, have, would.
so_orderWord order: So do I. NOT So I do. Neither can I. NOT Neither I can. You can also say Nor instead of Neither.
subjSchool subjects: art, foreign languages, geography, history, IT (information technology), literature, maths, PE (physical education), science (physics, chemistry and biology).
nounsMany nouns are verb + -ion, -sion, -ation or -ition: decide → decision, invite → invitation, compete → competition, pronounce → pronunciation. Some are new words: choose → choice, advise → advice, fly → flight, live → life, die → death, succeed → success.
similboth (We both like jazz), the same … as (the same eyes as you), look like + noun (She looks like her brother), similar to, different from, identical.
ppast_formPast perfect: had + past participle. Same form for all persons: I’d forgotten. She hadn’t seen it. Had you been there before?
ppast_useUse the past perfect for an action that happened BEFORE another action in the past: When I got to the station, the train had left (it left before I arrived).
ppast_d’d can mean had or would. had + past participle (I’d finished). would + infinitive (I’d like).
rep_tenseIn reported speech the verb usually goes one tense back: am / is → was, are → were, present simple → past simple, present continuous → past continuous, will → would, can → could, past simple and present perfect → past perfect.
rep_pronPronouns and possessives change too: ‘I’m tired,’ she said. → She said (that) she was tired. ‘my mum’ → her mum.
rep_say_telltell + a person: He told me (that) he was leaving. say without a person: He said (that) he was leaving. NOT He said me.
subjqWhen the question word (Who, What, Which, How many) is the SUBJECT of the verb, don’t use do / does / did: Who painted it? Which team won? How many people live here?
objqWhen the question word is NOT the subject, use do / does / did as normal: What did you buy? Who did you see? Where does she live?
timexsuddenly = quickly and unexpectedly. eventually = after a long time. meanwhile = while something else was happening. immediately / straight away = without delay.
saytelltell + a person (tell me, tell them) and tell a story, a joke, a lie, the truth. say + something (say sorry, say hello, say that …). You can say something to somebody. NOT say me.
qwordsQuestion words: who (person), whose (who it belongs to), what, which (from a few choices), where (place), when (time), why (reason), how (way), how many, how often, how long.
receptionCalling reception: Hello, this is room 613. There’s a problem with the air conditioning. I can’t get a signal. Receptionist: How can I help you? I’ll send somebody up to look at it. I’ll put you through to IT.
restaurantAt a restaurant: A table for four, please. Are you ready to order? I’ll have the steak, please. Can we have the bill, please? (American English: the check). Excuse me, I think there’s a mistake.
directionsDirections: Go straight on. Turn left / right. Take the first / second turning on the right. Turn right at the traffic lights. Go round the roundabout and take the third exit.
phoneOn the phone: Can I speak to …, please? I’ll put you through. She’s not at her desk. Can I leave a message? I’ll call back later. The line’s busy. Do you want to hold? I have the wrong number.
