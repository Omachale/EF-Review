c_willIn the other part of the sentence, use will / won’t + infinitive: … we’ll stay at home.
c_negNegative after if: don’t / doesn’t + verb (If you don’t find a taxi …).
c_matchFirst conditional: if + present simple, will / won’t + infinitive.
pp_adjPossessive adjectives come before a noun: my book, your coat, their car.
pp_proPossessive pronouns stand alone, with no noun after them: It’s mine. (mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs)
adv_lyMost adverbs: adjective + -ly (quiet → quietly, sad → sadly).
adv_yAdjectives ending in -y: change y to -ily (angry → angrily, lazy → lazily).
adv_ful-ful adjectives: add -ly (careful → carefully, beautiful → beautifully).
adv_irrIrregular: good → well. fast → fast. hard → hard.
adv_useAdjective + noun (a careful driver). Verb + adverb (he drives carefully).
num_bigNo -s on hundred, thousand or million after a number (two thousand, six million). In British English, say and after hundred (or after thousand if there are no hundreds): a hundred and eighty-four, three thousand and twenty-five.
num_dateDates: the + ordinal number + of + month: the twenty-fifth of May, the first of June.
num_fracFractions: one third, two thirds, three quarters (-s when there is more than one).
num_decDecimals: say point. 9.2 = nine point two.
num_pc% = per cent (American English: percent). Seventy-five per cent (no -s).
num_money$6,000,000 = six million dollars: million without -s, dollars with -s.
num_approxabout = more or less. at least = that number or more. between … and … = in the middle of two numbers.
oppLearn verbs in pairs of opposites: borrow / lend, push / pull, win / lose, buy / sell, send / receive …
backverb + back = return something, or return to a place: come back, go back, give back, pay back, send back, take back. call back = phone someone again.
abitUse a bit with negative adjectives and adverbs (a bit cold, a bit expensive, a bit slowly), with words that describe a problem (a bit big), and with comparatives (a bit better). Not with very positive adjectives: NOT a bit wonderful.
abit_forma bit + adjective (a bit big). Not a bit of big, not bit big.
prepLearn adjectives with their prepositions: afraid of, proud of, full of, tired of, good at, bad at, interested in, famous for, worried about, keen on, similar to, married to, nice to.
getget has many meanings: get lost (become), get a present (receive), get tickets (buy), get home (arrive).
win_earnwin a game, a match, a prize. earn money by working.
wear_carrywear clothes, shoes, glasses. carry something in your hands or arms.
meet_knowmeet = see someone for the first time (or see by arrangement). know = have known someone for some time (I’ve known her for ten years).
watch_lookwatch TV, a film, a match (something that moves). look at a photo, a picture (something that doesn’t move).
look_likelook + adjective (You look tired). look like + noun (She looks like her mother).
find_lookforlook for = try to find. find = discover something you were looking for.
say_telltell someone something (tell me, tell a joke, tell the truth). say something (say that …, say sorry). Not say me.
lend_borrowlend TO someone (I lent him £10). borrow FROM someone (I borrowed £10 from him).
hope_wait_expecthope = want something to happen. wait (for) = stay until something happens. expect = think something will happen.
miss_losemiss a bus, a train, a plane, a class. lose your keys, your glasses, a match.
listen_hearlisten to = pay attention to a sound. hear = a sound comes to your ears, even if you don’t try.
bringbring = to the place where the speaker is (Did you bring me a present?). take = to another place, away from the speaker.
shopUseful shopping words: size, changing room, receipt, try on, refund.
illheadache = pain in your head. temperature = your body is too hot. cough = noise from your chest. bad stomach = pain in your stomach.
pharmAt the pharmacy: What are your symptoms? Are you allergic to anything? Take one every four hours.
